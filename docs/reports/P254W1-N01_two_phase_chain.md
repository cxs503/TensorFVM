M1 solves the 1-D drift-flux equations for an air-water upflow in a vertical pipe with a 3 bar inlet and a 3 m length: the gas expands as the pressure falls, so the void fraction `alpha(z)` is not uniform. M2 is the Martin & Moyce (1952) dam break (column half-width a = 0.05715 m, aspect ratio 2) with the liquid fraction transported by a PLIC reconstruction. M3 is the Rayleigh-Plesset free oscillation of a 0.1 mm bubble in water plus the advection of a bubble cloud through a low-pressure duct. M4 is the one-phase Stefan problem for an ice-like medium (k = 2.22 W/m/K, L = 3.337e5 J/kg) solidified from a wall held 20 K below the fusion temperature.

## 2. Method

The four solvers are independent of the references:

* **M1**: the reference is the *closed-form* Wallis void fraction `alpha = (B - sqrt(B^2 - 4 v_dj j_g))/(2 v_dj)`, `B = C0(j_g+j_l) + v_dj`, with the pressure profile integrated by DOP853 at `rtol = 1e-12`; the numerical model is a conservative finite-volume march of the gas mass `alpha rho_g` with upwind face fluxes and a quasi-steady momentum balance.
* **M2**: PLIC reconstruction (Youngs normal, closed-form line constant) with geometric face fluxes, direction-split sweeps and a conservative bound limiter; momentum from the verified Phase-0 projection kernel. Front and column height are extracted with two pre-declared depth thresholds.
* **M3**: the Plesset linearisation `X'' + 2 beta X' + omega^2 X = 0` (`omega^2 = (3 gamma p_g0 - 2 sigma/R0)/(rho R0^2)`, `beta = 2 mu/(rho R0^2)`) is the reference; the numerical solution is the full nonlinear ODE integrated at `rtol = 1e-12`. The void transport advects the bubble number density and the gas mass conservatively (MUSCL-Hancock) and adds the growth source.
* **M4**: the reference is the Carslaw & Jaeger similarity solution `s = 2 lambda sqrt(alpha t)` with `sqrt(pi) lambda exp(lambda^2) erf(lambda) = St` solved to machine precision; the numerical model is an explicit enthalpy-method finite volume with the exact enthalpy inversion (no mushy band).

Every measured number is written to a SQLite ledger with its comparator and limit; this report is generated by querying that ledger, so no number is transcribed by hand.

## 5. Notes, limitations and pitfalls

* **J2 is an honest FAIL.** The surge front lags the digitised Martin & Moyce curve at early times and the deficit shrinks with time (max front error 0.9882 - over the whole window, 0.24 - for t* >= 3). The transport is not the limiting factor: the algebraic FCT scheme on the same grid gives 0.986 - and the interface is *thicker* (algebraic 3.121 cells vs PLIC 3.635 cells), so the deficit is in the first-order upwind momentum advection of the shared projection kernel (the Phase-0 gate reports the same failure). Fixing it needs a second-order momentum scheme, which is out of scope for this node and is left as the documented follow-up.
* The Martin & Moyce data are a digitisation of a published curve (few-percent reading error); the comparison therefore cannot resolve better than a few percent even with a perfect solver.
* The drift-flux pipe is frictionless (the verification target is the void-fraction profile, which wall friction does not enter); `C0 = 1.2` and the Wallis churn-turbulent drift velocity are closures, not first-principles results.
* The cavitation transport uses the inertia-controlled growth asymptote (no bubble oscillation at the transport time scale, period ~30 us); the full Rayleigh-Plesset dynamics is verified separately in M3.
* The Stefan solver assumes equal solid/liquid density and a semi-infinite domain whose length is about 15 times the final front position, so the front never approaches the far boundary.
