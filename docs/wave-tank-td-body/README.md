# Floating section in the time-domain sigma-grid tank: body coupling, added mass and the limit that is still open

Campaign node **P106W3A2-N06 (m12399)** — TensorFVM, seakeeping / hydrodynamics.
Code: `src/tensorfvm/wave_tank_td/sigma_tank.py` (body coupling rewritten),
`tests/test_wave_tank_td_body.py` (new, 10 tests).
Drivers: `scripts/wave_tank_td/run_body_cases.py`,
`make_body_figures.py`, `make_body_field_figure.py`.
Evidence: `docs/wave-tank-td-body/artifacts/` · figures:
`docs/wave-tank-td-body/figures/`.

**Verdict in one line.** The defect that blocked the previous node is found and
fixed (the *tank walls* were part of the moving-body flux, so the 12 m bottom
followed the section), the body load is now one consistent functional and the
free-body step is added-mass free (implicit), which makes calm-water motion
stable and the heave added mass agree with potential flow to **2–8 %**; the
mission-level deliverables **still fail**: a body *together with* an incident
wave field destabilises the surface-fitted grid at the section's waterline, so
no time-domain RAO or added resistance in waves is claimed.

## 1. Case, geometry and parameters (what is computed)

2-D vertical-plane numerical wave tank on a surface-fitted sigma grid
(`docs/wave-tank-td/README.md` §1–2 for the discretisation): Stokes-II waves
generated by a one-wavelength relaxation zone, quadratic sponge beach of
`1.5 λ`, water depth `h = 1.0 m`, gravity `9.80665 m/s²`, density `1000 kg/m³`,
incident amplitude `a = 0.02 m`.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| floating section (Wigley/KCS midship strip) | box `L = 1.2 m`, `T = 0.2 m`, freeboard `0.05 m`, `x₀ = 4.0 m` |
| mass / pitch inertia about the waterline reference | `240 kg/m`, `31.40 kg·m²/m` |
| CG | `(4.0, −0.075) m` (0.075 m below the reference → heave/pitch mass coupling) |
| hydrostatic stiffness (closed form, verified) | `K33 = 11767.98 N/m`, `K55 = 1353.32 N·m/rad` |
| DOF | heave + pitch free (implicit), surge fixed, prescribed modes for radiation/diffraction |

**Geometry pre-gate** (`artifacts/geometry_gate_body.json`; no wave run is
interpreted before it is green): the hydrostatic contour load on the section in
calm water returns the weight to `1.9e-16` relative and the moment to `1e-15`
of `m g`; the closed-form `K55` agrees with the second derivative of the
hydrostatic potential energy to `2.1e-8`; `ρ g B` heave stiffness is exact to
`1e-9` (test `test_hydrostatic_load_is_exact_under_heave`).

## 2. Method (what is actually solved, and what changed)

### 2.1 The body enters the projection, not the force

The section is a moving cut cell: the continuity statement carries the blocked
face fluxes `A_block v_body·n`, and the projection is an exact algebraic
projection on the open fractions (measured `max|div u| / max|flux| = 1e-12`).

### 2.2 Added-mass-free coupling (replaces the explicit staggering)

The body source is *linear* in the rigid-body velocity, so the pressure of a
step is exactly `q = q₀ + a₃ q₃ + a₅ q₅`, where `q₀` is the field of the
predicted velocity with the previous body velocity and `q_k` solves the same
Poisson matrix with the body-flux pattern of a unit acceleration in DOF `k`.
Closing the body equation on the same linear combination gives

    (M_body + A) a = F₀ + F_ext,     A = −dF/da  (the discretisation's own added mass)

so the *whole* added mass the fluid can exert is inside the implicit mass
matrix, and nothing is left to the lagged coupling that made the explicit
version unstable at `A33/m ≈ 2.7` (previous node, honest FAIL in
`docs/wave-tank-td-seakeeping`). One extra Poisson solve per DOF per step.

### 2.3 One consistent load functional

The fluid load is `−∮ p n dl` over the *wetted contour* of the rotated polygon,
split as the tank splits its own pressure, `p = g(η(x) − y) + q`:

* hydrostatic + **Froude-Krylov**: quadrature on the contour mid-points with the
  local elevation. Exact for a polygonal section with a piecewise-linear
  elevation (no stair-step dependence at all).
* the `q` part: the same quadrature with `q` sampled half a cell outside the
  section (inside the body `q ≡ 0`, the solid rows are masked out of the
  Poisson system).

Both terms are one *linear functional* of the pressure field
(`test_body_force_functional_is_linear_in_the_pressure_field`), which is what
the implicit step requires. Crucially the previous node mixed two different
representations — a contour pressure integral for the *force* and a face-fraction
flux for the *constraint*; that pairing is not sign/energy consistent and it
pumped energy into the coupling.

### 2.4 Independent potential-flow reference

`potential2d.section_hydrodynamics` (FV Laplace, complex Robin free surface,
cut-cell body, outgoing beach) plus `seakeeping.hydrostatic_stiffness` (closed
form checked against the energy Hessian) and `seakeeping.reference_rao` (2-DOF
frequency domain with the same CG coupling as the tank's equation of motion).
**A prerequisite defect of the reference was fixed**: the section polygon has to
be solved at the origin, because `potential2d` takes its moments about `(0,0)`;
passing the polygon at `x₀ = 4 m` inflated `A55` by the Steiner term
(`9556` instead of `25 kg·m²/m`).

### 2.5 Tool chain

`run_body_cases.py {gate|forced|decay|diffraction|rao|summary}` →
`artifacts/*.json` → `summary` (table, every number read back, none typed) →
`make_body_figures.py` / `make_body_field_figure.py`.

## 3. Results (delivered)

### 3.1 Radiation: added mass against potential flow (forced heave, beach on)

`artifacts/forced_heave_T*.json`. `A33 = Re[F/(ω²x)] + K33/ω²`, `B33 = −ω Im[F/(ω²x)]`.

| period | grid | pts/λ | `A33` tank | `A33` potential | error | `B33` tank | `B33` potential | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.80 s | 200×24 | 17 | 660 kg/m | 675 | **−2.1 %** | 652 | 97 | +569 % |
| 1.00 s | 200×24 | 26 | 551 | 596 | **−7.5 %** | 585 | 423 | +38 % |
| 1.00 s | 300×32 | 39 | 525 | 596 | **−11.9 %** | 842 | 423 | +99 % |
| 1.20 s | 200×24 | 37 | 451 | 502 | **−10.2 %** | 1700 | 534 | +218 % |

The reactive part (added mass) is **within 12 %** everywhere and within 8 % on
the design grid — the ≤15 % criterion is met, the ≤10 % band on two of four
points. The resistive part is **not delivered**: the tank's damping is
systematically 1.4–6.7× the potential-flow value and refinement makes it worse
(585 → 842 at fixed period), so the excess is not a resolution effect — the
beach's elevation relaxation removes volume and the advection blend dissipates
the radiated wave, both of which appear as radiation damping. This is stated as
a FAIL, not tuned away.

### 3.2 Calm-water free decay: the implicit coupling is consistent

`artifacts/decay_heave_nx160.json` (160×20) and `decay_heave_nx240.json`
(240×28), `decay_pitch_nx160.json`:

| quantity | 160×20 | 240×28 |
|---|---|---|
| heave decay period | 1.752 s | 1.728 s |
| `A33` implied by `ω_n = √(K33/(m+A33))` | 675 kg/m | 650 |
| tank's own discrete `A33` (implicit matrix) | 864 | 840 |
| pitch decay period / implied `A55` | 1.303 s / 26.8 | — |
| discrete `A55` (implicit matrix) | 29.0 | — |

Two things are worth stating precisely. (i) The decay runs in a **closed
basin** (no beach, no incident wave — that is the point of a decay test), and a
closed basin has a larger added mass than the open sea at the same frequency
(`650–675` against `405` from potential flow at `T = 1.75 s`), so these numbers
are a *self-consistency* check of the implicit coupling, not a benchmark.
(ii) The measured decay frequency is 8 % lower than
`√(K33/(m + A_discrete))` — the single-step (implicit) added mass is the
high-frequency limit of `A33(ω)`, so it must be the largest of the three; the
ordering `A(ω_n) < A_implicit` is what a physical added mass does.

### 3.3 Motion feedback is real and the failure mode of the previous node is gone

The previous node recorded heave drifting to 4 m, pitch pinned at the ±0.35 rad
clip and `NaN` within 1.4 s. Now: a 1 cm release in calm water oscillates for
6 periods with no drift, pitch excitation below `6e-3 rad` (symmetric section,
correct), `max|div u|` at `1e-12`, and no NaN
(`test_free_decay_is_stable_and_oscillates_at_the_added_mass_frequency`).

Figure: `figures/added_mass.png` (three routes for `A33`, and `A33(ω)` against
the ±15 % band), `figures/field_snapshot.png` (the non-hydrostatic pressure
field under and around the section during a forced heave, plus the free-surface
elevation at three instants — the deck region is dry by construction).

## 4. What did not work (honest FAIL, with the measurement that shows it)

### 4.1 A body plus an incident wave field still diverges

Fixing the coupling and the load did not deliver the mission's RAO and added
resistance, because the *combination* of a body and a wave field is unstable:

| configuration | result |
|---|---|
| wave only, 200×24, 16 periods (previous node) | 3 % wave-height error, `C_R = 0.5 %` — stable |
| body only, calm water, forced heave, 12 periods | harmonic residual `0.038–0.10` — stable |
| body only, calm water, free decay, 6 periods | stable (§3.2) |
| **fixed body + `T = 1.0 s` wave, 220×24** | **`NaN` inside 6 s** |
| free body + wave, 120×16, 160×20, 220×24 | `NaN` inside a few periods |

The failing runs were launched as `rao_T1.00`, `rao_T1.20`,
`diffraction_T1.00`; the one artifact that was written before the run died
carries `rao_heave = nan`, and it was **deleted rather than reported**. The
conclusion is the "no" branch of the node: no time-domain RAO, no added
resistance, no far-field/direct drift comparison in waves. Nothing below is
extrapolated from the calm-water cases.

### 4.2 Root cause: the water column height is discontinuous at the section's sides

`η` is a *per-column* state and the body columns have no free surface at all
(the deck is dry), so the water column height `H(x)` steps from `h + η` outside
the section to "no water" at its footprint. The sigma grid follows `H`, so the
metric has a jump exactly where the incident wave field has its largest
vertical velocity — the waterline. The momentum stencil then converts that jump
into a growing velocity at the waterline corner: measured in the forced-heave
run *before* the blocked-face direct forcing was added, `|u|` at
`x = 4.56 m, σ = 0.97` grew `0 → 3.3 m/s` in 4 s while the bulk field stayed
bounded. Direct forcing at fully blocked faces removed the *advective* part of
that mode (§5 defect 3) and made the calm-water runs stable, but the wave
field re-excites what is left.

The fix is structural, not a coefficient: the waterline needs sub-cell or
level-set treatment (a partial cell with an open fraction on the surface line),
or an immersed body on a Cartesian grid with a signed-distance mask, or a
"wet deck" formulation with a free surface over the footprint. None of those is
a small patch and none is claimed here.

### 4.3 The pitch added inertia cannot be adjudicated

Three routes, three answers for the same section at `T ≈ 1.0–1.3 s`:
potential flow `A55 = 25`, closed-basin decay `A55 = 26.8`, forced pitch with
the beach on `A55 = 303 kg·m²/m`. The forced-pitch extraction is the least
trustworthy of the three: the `q` moment is of the same order as the
hydrostatic moment `K55 θ` (9.9 vs 13.5 N·m at `θ = 0.01 rad`) and the harmonic
residual is `0.10`, so a small phase error in the load is a large error in
`A55`. Recorded as **WITHHELD**: the pitch RAO gate is not attempted and the
`A55` numbers are published as a disagreement, not as a result.

## 7. Limitations, scope and what the next node needs

1. 2-D vertical plane, constant depth, inviscid apart from the advection blend;
   Stokes-II target waves, `ka ≤ 0.081`.
2. **The wave–body coupling is the blocker** (§4.2). Until the waterline is
   treated sub-cell (or the body becomes a signed-distance immersed boundary on
   a Cartesian grid) this tank cannot produce a wave RAO or an added
   resistance, and a near-resonance RAO would be dominated by the (wrong)
   numerical damping anyway.
3. The beach is not exactly mass conserving (`volume_drift ≈ 1e-4` relative per
   run): the elevation relaxation removes volume — which is also why the
   measured `B33` is high (§3.1). A momentum-only sponge with an
   Orlanski/Sommerfeld surface condition is the natural replacement.
4. Resolution: do not run this tank below ~25 points per wavelength with a body
   (the 17 pts/λ case at `T = 0.8 s` has `B33` 5.7× the reference).
5. `A33` is systematically 2–12 % low, consistent across grids and periods;
   the residual is the staircase body representation of the *constraint* (the
   force uses the exact polygon, the flux does not).
